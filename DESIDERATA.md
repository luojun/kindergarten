# Desiderata for the Kindergarten

### 1. Ordinary Live-In and Play-In

1. *A live-in environment* that provides for basic survival needs: Maintaining adequate battery charge consistutes survival or "continual living".
2. *A "play-in" environment* that affords *ample opportunity for trying*.
3. *All time is demo time*: just come in person or go to the live cast.


### 2. Learning from Experience

1. Ordinary live-in and play-in means *temporal uniformity*: every moment is continuation of living, playing, and learning.
2. Successes, failures, deaths, and births may occur, but *environment never resets*.


### 3. Part of the Big World

1. *Being in the kindergarten is being in the big world*, without any moat separating the agent from its environment and without any possibility of representing the environment fully.
2. *All events are part of the environment dynamics*, including accidents and recoveries, interventions and resumptions that may or may not be experiencable.
3. *All actors are part of the environment*, including (robot) caretakers, researchers, and visitors.


### 4. Instrumentation and Automation as Interaction

1. *Instrumentation is interaction with the kindergarteners*, insofar as the information flow leads to an experiencable change, even if through humans and caretakers.
2. *Automation, e.g. automated recovery, is interaction with the kindergarteners*, even if not the focus of the research.
3. As passive remote monitoring, *telemetry is the norm*; as active remote control, through *teleoperation is the exception*.


### 5. Simulation for Reality Augmentation rather than Reality Simplification

1. The kindergarten is an *ordinary real-world environment that promotes continual learning* while remaining agnostic to specific solutions.
2. Simulated versions and digital twins of the kindergartens are *for augmenting reality to promote understanding, not for approximating reality to simplify the problem*.


### 6. Open

1. *Doors Open*: keep the kindergarten open as part of ordinariy, social life -- bring yourself, bring your friends, bring your own bots (BYOB).
2. *Design Open*: design is extensible and composable, design specs are open -- bring your own design "mixins".
3. *Source Open*: infrastructure and instrumentation software fully open sourced under permissive licence -- bring your own code.


### 7. Concrete

1. Offering specific robot design: *Open Ant*.
2. Offering specific environment design: *Tessalia*.
3. These designs can be readily and affordably built and maintained.


### 8. Ecological Layering

1. The kindergarten is layered in a naturally ecological order: *crib, play pen, play ground, school yard, and beyond*.
2. The ecological layering affords an *auto-curriculum*: a robot start in the crib in which charging is automated could learn to play with its own body and then learn to climb out of the crib into the play pen, and so on.
3. *"Foot traffic" crosscuts the layering* to engender highly variable dynamics: humans, robot caregivers (e.g. a mobile manipulator), and other "kindergarteners" could also move across the layers quickly as forms of ordinary activities.
